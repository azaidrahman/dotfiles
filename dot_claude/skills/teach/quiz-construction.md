# Quiz construction

Read this before you write any multiple-choice question with `AskUserQuestion`.

## Why a procedure

A post-hoc check for "even options" is not enough. You write a good answer and some throwaway wrong options, and you do not look at them again. The tell is in the options before any check runs. So build the options so that evenness is automatic.

## The procedure

1. **Every option is a bare claim.** Put no justification in any option. The most common giveaway is a correct option that carries its own reason ("..., because it keeps X") next to bare distractors. All reasoning goes in your next message, after the learner answers.
2. **Write the correct claim first. Then mutate it into each distractor.** Take one specific misconception, or one neighbour that is easy to confuse, and state what a person who holds it would claim. Use the same sentence skeleton, the same grain size, and the same register as the correct claim. Now every option is "the claim under some belief". The correct one is the claim under the correct belief.
3. **Each distractor is a real error.** The learner must be able to make it. Which distractor the learner picks tells you which gap to teach into. A distractor must also be wrong without doubt on the intended reading. Tempting, not tricky.
4. **No asymmetric emphasis.** Do not bold or quote the key term in one option only. Bold nothing, or bold the parallel term in every option.
5. **Always add a literal `I don't know` option.** Put it last. It is a distinct signal: the learner did not guess. Treat it as a genuine gap, never as a wrong answer.
6. **Ask exactly one question per call.** Several small adapted questions beat one big question with caveats.

## The final check

Read the finished options as a person who does not know the material. If you can still tell which option is right, you skipped step 1 or step 2. Rewrite the set. Do not patch it.

## After the answer

In your next message, and in this order:
- `✓` or `✗`, or `I don't know` noted as a gap
- the correct option
- one short explanation of why it is correct, and why the chosen distractor is wrong if the learner missed
